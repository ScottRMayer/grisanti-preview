A defense built
from the other side of the case file.

Most attorneys learn the law from the outside looking in. Joseph D. Grisanti spent years working it from the inside — first as a Special Agent with the Federal Bureau of Investigation, then as a Prosecutor, before opening his Brick, New Jersey practice.

That trajectory shapes everything about how this firm approaches a case. Police reports get read the way an investigator wrote them. Frequently Asked

Questions worth
answering up front.

Quick answers to what clients most often ask before picking up the phone. For anything specific to your situation, call — the consultation is free.

What happens during a free initial consultation?
You speak directly with Attorney Grisanti — not an intake screener. We listen to the facts, identify the legal questions in play, and give you an honest read on whether the matter is worth pursuing and what representation would cost. There is no obligation to retain the firm after the consultation.
How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in New Jersey?
New Jersey sets specific statutes of limitations for personal injury claims, and the deadlines are short — generally two years from the date of injury for most negligence claims, with different rules for workers' compensation, claims against public entities, and other categories. Because exceptions and shortened windows apply, call promptly to confirm the deadline that applies to your situation.
What is the attorney review period in a New Jersey real estate contract?
Most residential real estate contracts in New Jersey include a three-business-day attorney review period after the contract is signed by both buyer and seller. During this window, either party's attorney can disapprove, modify, or terminate the contract. Using this period properly is one of the main reasons New Jersey buyers and sellers retain attorneys for residential closings.
Do I need a lawyer for a first-offense DWI in New Jersey?
A DWI conviction carries fines, surcharges, possible ignition-interlock requirements, and downstream consequences for insurance, employment, and travel. Even a first offense is heavily fact-dependent — police reports, calibration records, observation periods, and Alcotest procedures all create avenues for defense. Speak with an attorney before deciding how to plead.
What is the difference between a will and a living will?
A last will and testament directs how your assets are distributed and who serves as guardian of minor children after your death. A living will (also called an advance directive) gives instructions about medical treatment if you become unable to make decisions for yourself. They serve different purposes — most complete estate plans include both, along with a power of attorney.
What does "no legal fee unless we recover" mean for a personal injury case?
On a contingency-fee personal injury matter, the firm's legal fee is taken as a percentage of any recovery — if there is no recovery, there is no legal fee. Case costs (filing fees, expert witnesses, medical records, court reporters, etc.) are separate from fees and may still be the client's responsibility depending on the engagement. The specifics are spelled out in the written retainer agreement reviewed with you before representation begins.
